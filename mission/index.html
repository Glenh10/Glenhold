Why I speak openly about my faith at Glenhold

I hesitated before putting my Catholic faith on Glenhold’s website. I take seriously the difference between sharing my faith and using it to win business. I also know how easy it is to call a business Christian and how much harder it is to live up to that word.

I decided to speak openly because I want to share the Gospel in every part of my life. My wife and I take our two boys to Mass, and we’re raising them to understand that what we believe on Sunday has a claim on how we spend Monday. That includes the hours I spend at my desk.

I believe God can use ordinary work to make us holy. For me, that often comes down to what I do when a client would never know the difference. Do I take the time to find out why an account is off? If I’ve made a mistake, do I pick up the phone and own it? Those decisions are part of what it means for me to practice my faith as a bookkeeper.

The Christian economy I want to help build

I’d like it to be easier for Christians to find one another, hire one another, and send work each other’s way. I think of what it means for a family when a contractor has enough steady work to bring on an apprentice, or for a parish when a local business owner can afford to be generous year after year. That’s what I have in mind when I talk about a stronger Christian economy.

My trade is accounting. After working at PwC and going on to lead accounting work for businesses, I started Glenhold so I could work directly with owners. When someone asks, “Can I afford to put another person on payroll?” I want them to have books they can trust and a person they can call who knows their business. That’s the contribution I can make.

Jack
Owner, Glenhold Bookkeeping

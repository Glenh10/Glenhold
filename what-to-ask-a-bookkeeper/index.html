What to Ask Before Hiring a Bookkeeper

A good first conversation answers who does the work, what the price includes, and what happens when the books are a mess.

Book a Free Call

Ask these before you hire anyone

I'd rather you ask blunt questions on the first call than discover the answers in month three. Here's the list I'd use, and the way I answer each one if you ask me. You can use the questions with me or with someone else.

If you want the short version of my background first: I've been in accounting since 2021, including audit work at PwC, and I'm a Certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor. You can also read the about page and watch how I explain the work on YouTube at @BookkeepingBizBlueprint.

Who actually does the bookkeeping?

Ask for a name. Ask whether that person is the one who'll reply when a report looks wrong. A firm can be a fine choice, but you should know if your file will move between people you've never spoken to.

With Glenhold, the answer is me. You're not assigned to a queue. If I'm not the right capacity for the volume you have, I'll say that before we start.

What does the monthly price include?

Get the included work in plain language: which accounts are reconciled, which reports you receive, and whether questions between closes are part of the fee. “Full-service bookkeeping” isn't a scope.

On my monthly bookkeeping plans, the work is categorization, bank and credit card reconciliation, a profit and loss statement, a balance sheet, and email support. I aim for the 15th of the following month, once statements and answers are in. Essential starts at $350, Core at $450, and Strategic at $700. The pricing page and the cost guide show what moves a quote between those starting points.

What would change the price later?

Ask what happens if you add a card, open a second location, turn on payroll, or double the transactions. A price that can't be explained will get explained later as a surprise.

I quote from volume, the number of accounts, and the reporting. If the scope changes, we talk before the bill changes. Cleanup of old months isn't quietly added to the monthly fee. It's its own quote.

What if the books are already behind?

Ask whether they'll start “next month” and leave the history, or whether they can rebuild the periods you need. Ask how they price that rebuild.

I do both, and I don't blend them. Cleanup and catch-up is scoped from the records. Monthly work starts once we know what current means. If you're looking at the warning signs, the catch-up guide is the practical version.

Which software, and whose file is it?

Ask where the books live, who owns the login, and what you leave with if you stop. You shouldn't have to beg for your own history.

I work in QuickBooks Online, and the file is yours. If you'd rather keep doing the entry yourself, setup and training is a separate offer. I'm not going to lock the books to a system you can't open.

When do the reports show up, and will anyone explain them?

A report you don't understand isn't doing you any good. Ask when it arrives and whether you can ask what a number means.

I send a profit and loss statement and a balance sheet, and you can ask me about them. If you want a standing walkthrough, we put that in the scope instead of assuming it. What those statements are for is written out if you want it before we talk.

Do you work with my tax preparer, and do you file the return?

These are different questions. Bookkeeping can be ready for a CPA and still not include the tax return. Ask which one they are selling.

I organize the books and the reports. I don't prepare or file the tax return. I'm glad to coordinate with the preparer you already trust. CPA-ready books are that handoff, not a tax-prep engagement.

What do you need from me each month?

A bookkeeper who needs nothing from you is guessing. Ask what you have to send, and how those files should be shared. The website form is a fine place for a first note. It's a bad place for bank statements.

I need statements for the accounts in scope, and answers when a transaction could be two different things. The checklist is what a handoff looks like. I'll tell you how to send sensitive records before you send them.

How do we start, and how do we stop?

Ask what the first month looks like, and what you keep if you leave. A long contract is a fact you should hear before you sign, not after.

We agree on scope, timing, and price before I start. Love your first month of monthly bookkeeping, or that month is free. Cleanup and training are outside that guarantee. If monthly work ends, you keep the QuickBooks file and the reports that were already delivered. I don't hold the books hostage.

Bring this to the call

You don't need a perfect file. You need a description: what the business does, how many accounts, whether payroll or loans are in the picture, and whether you're current. Service businesses, contractors, home services, and professional practices can all start from that same list.

More of the standard answers are on the bookkeeping FAQ. When you want to ask me directly, book a free call, call (805) 419-3451, email jack@glenhold.com, or use the form. I reply within one business day.

Common questions

Should price be part of the first conversation?

Yes. Ask what's included, what's quoted separately, and what would move the monthly price up or down. A clear scope is part of the price.

How do I tell if a bookkeeper understands my business?

Describe how you get paid, how you pay people, and which reports you actually use. A careful bookkeeper will tell you what they need to see before quoting, and they'll say so if the work is outside what they do.

Bookkeeping for home service businesses

Home-service bookkeeping follows the truck and the invoice. I categorize parts, fuel, and labor-related costs, reconcile the accounts, and send a profit and loss and a balance sheet, usually by the 15th. Plans start at $349. You work with me.

Book a Free Call

Plumbers, electricians, HVAC crews, landscapers, cleaners, and the other trades that work in people's houses share a bookkeeping shape. Money comes in from invoices and driveway card readers. Money goes out for parts, fuel, dumps, insurance, and the van. I build the categories around that shape so the month answers a simple question: after the parts and the truck, what did the work earn?

The card in the truck is where these files get muddy. Parts, fuel, and a stop for lunch can share a statement. I ask you to flag what you already know is personal. The rest gets coded to the job cost it belongs to. A transfer to your personal account is a draw. A part you bought with personal cash and want the company to carry is money you contributed.

If you use a field app that invoices and takes cards, I want the payout report next to the bank deposit. The fee stays visible. Sales tax you collected, when the job includes it, is a liability at the statewide base of 7.25 percent plus any district tax for the job location. I look up the rate that applies. Your preparer files the return.

Catch-up is common in this work because the month gets finished in the driveway, and the books wait. I quote the open months from the statements and the app exports. Then the monthly plan keeps the new months from stacking up. I've been in accounting since 2021, including audit work at PwC, and I'm a Certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor.

If you run two trucks or two trades under one company, say so. I can keep the costs visible by crew when the memos support it, and the company profit and loss still closes as one month.

A home-service close

  1. Name the apps and the accounts. Invoicing tool, card reader, operating account, and the truck card.
  2. Match payouts to deposits. Gross, fees, and the net that hit the bank.
  3. Code the truck card. Parts, fuel, and the personal items you flag.
  4. Record the van and the insurance. So those costs stay visible every month.
  5. Send the 15th reports. Profit and loss and balance sheet, with a call on the Strategic plan.

Bring this to the first call

  • The field app or invoicing tool you use
  • Truck cards and the operating account
  • A recent payout report
  • Vehicle or equipment loans you want tracked
  • How far behind the books are

Common questions

I am a one-truck company. Is that enough to hire you?

Yes. Essential starts at $349 a month for straightforward books, and a single truck is a common place to begin.

Customers pay by card in the driveway. How do you record it?

I reconcile the reader or app report to the deposit, including the fee the processor kept.

Can you separate maintenance work from install work?

Yes, when you can tell those jobs apart on the invoice or the memo. I follow that split.

What about the van payment?

I can record the payment and, when you want the balance sheet to show the loan, split principal and interest.

Who will I hear from?

You hear from me. The same person closes the month and answers the question about a part that looked personal.

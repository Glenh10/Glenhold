Bookkeeping for medical and dental practices

Practice bookkeeping is the business side of the office: insurance deposits, patient payments, rent, supplies, equipment, and owner draws. I categorize those, reconcile the accounts, and send a profit and loss and a balance sheet. I keep patient records out of the books and out of the contact form.

Book a Free Call

I keep the business books for the practice. Rent, supplies, equipment payments, insurance deposits, patient payments that arrive as a batch, payroll through your payroll company, and the draws you take are the transactions that change the reports. I categorize them and reconcile the accounts. Clinical records stay in your practice system.

An insurance deposit is a total. The explanation is the remittance or the billing export. I want that export beside the bank line so collections, adjustments, and the amount that actually arrived can be told apart. A card payment at the front desk gets the same treatment: the batch report and the deposit should agree.

Please keep patient names, treatment notes, and insurance cards out of the introductory form and out of the bookkeeping file. Account numbers for the practice's own bank, the vendor list, and the deposit reports are what I need. If a report includes patient detail, ask me how to share a version that shows the money.

Associates, hygiene payroll, and owner compensation each deserve their own presentation when you want to read them. Your payroll company runs payroll. On Strategic I record the journal entries. Equipment leases and practice loans can sit on the balance sheet with a balance you recognize. I've been in accounting since 2021, including audit work at PwC, and I'm a Certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor. You work with me, and the reports are aimed at the 15th.

A new associate or a new operatory changes the month. Tell me in the month it happens. I would rather show the change on purpose than leave you comparing two months that quietly mean different things.

How a practice month is closed

  1. List the business accounts. Operating, payroll, cards, and the loans. Patient records stay in the practice system.
  2. Pair each deposit with its report. Insurance remits and front-desk batches.
  3. Code overhead in plain groups. Rent, supplies, labs, equipment, insurance, and owner activity.
  4. Reconcile. Every business account ties to QuickBooks Online.
  5. Send the package your CPA can use. Profit and loss and balance sheet. They file the return.

What to gather, and what to leave out

  • Business bank, card, and loan accounts
  • A billing or remittance export that explains deposits
  • Payroll reports from your payroll company
  • The last closed month
  • Patient names and clinical files, which stay with you

Common questions

Do you need patient names?

Leave patient names and clinical records out of the books and out of the contact form. I work from deposits, reports, and account activity.

Insurance deposits never match a single day. Can you still reconcile?

Yes. I use the billing report that lists the payments inside the deposit and tie that total to the bank.

Can my CPA keep preparing the return?

Yes. I hand your CPA clean, reconciled books. They file the return.

What about equipment loans?

I record the payment and track the balance when you want it on the balance sheet.

Which plan do practices usually start on?

It depends on account volume and reporting. Essential starts at $349 a month. Many practices want Core for the quarterly view.

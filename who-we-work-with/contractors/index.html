Bookkeeping for contractors and trades

Contractor bookkeeping has to show the job, the materials, and the money you still owe or are still owed. I categorize those transactions, reconcile the bank and the cards, and send a profit and loss and a balance sheet, usually by the 15th. Plans start at $349. You work with me.

Book a Free Call

A contracting month moves through more accounts than it looks like from the truck. Materials, subcontractors, fuel, customer deposits, and the money you pull out for yourself can all hit the same checking account in one week. I separate them. The profit and loss then shows what the jobs earned, and the balance sheet shows deposits you are still holding and what the business owes.

I ask for a list of every card, including the one in the glove box. Transfers between your own accounts get labeled as transfers. A personal grocery stop on the business card becomes an owner draw. A payment you made from personal funds for a job becomes money you put back into the company. Those three labels are the difference between a month you can trust and a month that looks busy and says nothing.

If you want reporting by job, bring an example of the report you already wish you had. I will match the categories and the job names to that picture when the file can support it. If the jobs live only in your head, I start with solid company-level books and add job names as you go. The first call is where I quote that scope.

Open months are a catch-up, priced from the records. A current file goes on Essential, Core, or Strategic. Love your first month of books, or it's free. Reports are aimed at the 15th. I've been in accounting since 2021, including audit work at PwC, and I'm a Certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor.

How I take a trade through the month

  1. List the accounts and the cards. Operating account, tax savings, materials cards, and the personal account you sometimes use for the business.
  2. Mark deposits and draws. Customer money still tied to an open job, money you took out, and money you put back in.
  3. Code job costs. Materials, subcontractors, fuel, dumps, permits, and the tools you want to see on their own line.
  4. Reconcile. Bank and every card. A difference stays on a list until it has a name.
  5. Send the reports. Profit and loss and balance sheet. Job detail when that was part of the quote. A monthly call on Strategic.

What helps a contractor close

  • Account and card list, including closed cards from the period
  • Customer deposits you are still holding
  • Subcontractor payments and the jobs they belong to
  • Loan or vehicle statements if you want balances tracked
  • The last month you believe is finished

Common questions

Can you track costs by job?

Tell me the report you want to see. If the job names are in the file, I categorize to those jobs and you can read them on the profit and loss.

How should customer deposits be recorded?

Money you are still holding for a job sits as a liability until you earn it. I record it that way when you tell me the job is open.

The truck card mixes fuel and personal stops. Is that usable?

Yes. Flag the personal stops and I code them to owner draw so the job costs stay clean.

Do you run payroll for the crew?

Your payroll company runs payroll. On the Strategic plan I record the payroll journal entries in the books.

What does a small crew pay to start?

Essential starts at $349 a month. A busier set of accounts, or job reporting, may be Core or Strategic.

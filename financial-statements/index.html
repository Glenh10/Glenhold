How to read your financial statements

The profit and loss shows what the business earned and spent. The balance sheet shows what it owns, what it owes, and what you have taken out. I send both with the monthly close, aimed at the 15th.

Book a Free Call

A profit and loss and a balance sheet are the two reports I send with a monthly close. The profit and loss shows what the business earned and spent over a period. The balance sheet shows what it owns, what it owes, and the owner's stake on a single date. I want you to be able to read both without a glossary.

On the profit and loss, start at the top. Income should match the way you get paid: sales, fees you earned, or the channels you sell through. Costs come next. A contractor looks at materials and subcontractors. A restaurant looks at food and the processor fees. A practice looks at rent, supplies, and labor. The bottom of the report is the profit after those costs. If a number surprises you, the transaction list behind it is the next click, and I will point you to it.

The balance sheet is the snapshot. Cash should match the reconciled bank. Credit cards should match the statements. Sales tax you have collected and not yet sent sits as a liability. Customer deposits you are still holding sit as a liability too. Loans show the balance you still owe when loan tracking is part of the work. Owner draws reduce equity. They are money you took out. They are a balance-sheet movement.

I send these reports aimed at the 15th of the next month. Core adds a quarterly summary and a year-to-date comparison, which is how you see whether this March looks like last March. Strategic adds custom reports and a call so you and I can walk the lines that matter. The reports are built from reconciled accounts. That is what makes them worth reading.

A useful comparison needs the same categories in both months. That is why I keep account names steady once the file is settled. A new kind of income can have a new line. An old line keeps its name so last year and this year sit on the same row. If something in the business changed, tell me in the month it changed and I will show it on purpose. Bring the question you hope the report will answer, and I will make sure that line is easy to find.

I hand your CPA the same books. They file the return. If a lender has asked for a period of statements, I organize the months they named. I've been in accounting since 2021, including audit work at PwC, and I'm a Certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor. Now I bring that same care to small businesses around Simi Valley.

How to read the month I send

  1. Read income first. Does it match what you remember collecting?
  2. Read the costs you feel. Food, materials, rent, payroll, fees. The ones you talk about at the counter.
  3. Check the bottom. Profit after those costs, for the month and, on Core, for the year so far.
  4. Open the balance sheet. Cash, cards, tax you still owe, and loans.
  5. Ask about one line. Reply with the number that looks off. I would rather explain it than have you guess.

A reliable pair of statements has

  • A profit and loss for the month you asked about
  • A balance sheet dated the last day of that month
  • Bank and card balances that match the statements
  • Owner draws labeled as draws
  • A note listing anything still open

Common questions

Which reports do I get every month?

A profit and loss and a balance sheet. Core adds a quarterly summary and a year-to-date comparison.

What if cash on the balance sheet looks wrong?

It should match the reconciled bank. Reply with the line and I will trace it.

Where do owner draws show up?

On the balance sheet, as money you took out. They stay off the profit and loss.

Can I share these with my CPA?

Yes. I hand your CPA clean, reconciled books. They file the return.

When do they arrive?

I aim for the 15th of the next month, once statements and your answers are in.

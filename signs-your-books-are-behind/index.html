Signs Your Small Business Books Are Behind (and How to Catch Up)

Falling behind is common. The fix is a clear starting month, a catch-up scope, and a monthly close after that.

Book a Free Call

Behind usually looks ordinary

Most owners who call me are not ignoring the business. They are doing the work, and the bookkeeping is the task that lost the argument. Contractors, home-service crews, and professional practices tell me the same story: the month was busy, then another month was busy, and now QuickBooks feels like a place they do not want to open.

You do not need a dramatic failure to be behind. You need a file you would not hand to your tax preparer today.

Signs the books have slipped

  • You cannot name the last reconciled month. If the answer is “sometime last year,” or you are not sure what reconciled means in your file, the close has stopped.
  • The bank and the books disagree. The balance on the screen and the balance at the bank are different, and the difference has been there long enough that you have stopped investigating it.
  • Personal and business spending share an account. Draws, groceries, and job materials are in one feed, and sorting them has become a project you postpone.
  • A card or a loan never made it into the file. The profit and loss statement looks fine because a whole account is missing.
  • Deposits do not match what you think you earned. Processor fees, refunds, and transfers are still lumped together.
  • You only learn how the year went at tax time. There is no monthly profit number you use to make decisions.
  • Your tax preparer is waiting. They asked for reports, and building those reports would mean reconstructing the year.
  • Receipts live in a truck, a folder, or an inbox. The software is not the system. The pile is.
  • You are guessing before you hire, buy equipment, or move money. The books are not late in a technical sense only. They are late for the decision in front of you.

One of these can be a loose end. Several of them together means the file is behind, even if the business itself is doing fine.

What I would not do next

Do not spend a weekend force-feeding a year of transactions into software you do not trust. That usually creates duplicates, and the cleanup gets bigger. Do not email account numbers or statements through the form on this site. And do not wait for a “quiet week.” The quiet week is how the next quarter disappears.

How catch-up actually works

  1. Find the last month you believe. That might be the last reconciliation, or the last month your tax preparer already used. We start from somewhere solid.
  2. List the accounts. Checking, savings, cards, loans, and payment processors, including accounts you closed during the period. The handoff checklist is the short version.
  3. Scope the cleanup. Cleanup and catch-up is quoted from the periods, the accounts, and how much has to be corrected. It is not the same price as a calm month of bookkeeping.
  4. Rebuild what the records support. I categorize what I can, reconcile what I can, and ask you about the transfers and personal items I cannot see from a statement. I will not invent a story for a charge neither of us recognizes.
  5. Produce reports for the periods we agreed. Those are the financial statements you can read, and the package your tax preparer may be asking for. If a lender is the deadline, say so. CPA-ready books and loan-ready records are scoped around that request.
  6. Put a monthly close under it. Catch-up without monthly bookkeeping is how the file falls behind again. We can price both at once.

What it costs to get current

I cannot quote catch-up from this page. A few unfinished months on two accounts is a different project from a year of unreconciled cards and a processor you have never matched. Monthly work, once you are current, starts at $350, $450, or $700. That comparison is on the cost guide.

Mention the deadline on the first call. A tax filing date and a loan conversation are different schedules, and I would rather tell you what is realistic than promise a rush I cannot keep.

Common questions

How far behind is too far behind?

There is no magic number of months. If you cannot name the last reconciled month, or you would not trust a profit number, the books are behind enough to deal with.

Can you catch my books up?

Yes. Cleanup and catch-up can rebuild the periods we agree on, using the statements and records you have. The quote and the timeline depend on that scope.

What happens after the books are caught up?

The useful next step is monthly bookkeeping, so the file does not slip again. We can scope the monthly work at the same time as the catch-up.

What should I send in the first message?

Tell me the type of business, the accounts you use, and how far back the books go. Leave account numbers and statements out of the website form. If we work together, I will tell you a safe way to share records.

Bring unfinished bookkeeping up to date

Glenhold Bookkeeping helps small businesses work through months or years of unfinished records. Cleanup focuses on correcting books that already exist; catch-up focuses on recording periods that have not been completed. Your project may involve both.

Based in Simi Valley, Glenhold works with businesses across industries throughout nearby Ventura County and Los Angeles County communities. You can request a one-time cleanup or discuss continuing with monthly bookkeeping afterward.

What a cleanup project can cover

  • Organizing bank and credit card statements, transaction exports, and existing accounting records.
  • Importing missing transactions and classifying income and expenses.
  • Reviewing duplicates, unmatched transfers, and inconsistent account balances.
  • Reconciling bank and credit card accounts to the available statements.
  • Organizing the chart of accounts so financial reports are easier to use.
  • Preparing a clear handoff of the completed bookkeeping to your CPA or tax preparer.

The work and the records needed depend on the condition of your books. If an entry is unclear, the next step is to resolve it with you rather than guess its purpose.

What should you gather first?

Start with the periods that need attention, a list of every business bank and credit card account, and the statements for those accounts. Include accounts closed during the period. Loan statements, payroll reports, and records of owner contributions or withdrawals may also be relevant.

If the business changed banks, include both the old and new account records. Transfers are much easier to reconcile when both sides are available. See the bookkeeping handoff checklist for a more complete preparation guide.

How much does catch-up bookkeeping cost?

Cleanup and catch-up projects are quoted based on the number of periods, accounts, transactions, and corrections required. Monthly plan starting prices are not a price quote for historical cleanup. Jack will discuss the scope before you decide to proceed.

How long will it take?

Timing depends on the size of the backlog, missing records, and how quickly open questions can be answered. Mention any tax-preparer, lender, or internal deadline during the first conversation so the timeline can be assessed.

What happens when the cleanup is complete?

You can move to monthly bookkeeping to keep future periods current, or discuss training and periodic accuracy check-ins if you prefer to maintain the books yourself. Bookkeeping prepares your records; tax return preparation and filing should be coordinated with your tax professional.

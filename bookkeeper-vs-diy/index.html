Hiring a Bookkeeper vs. Doing It Yourself

Doing the books yourself can be the right call. It stops being the right call when the numbers slip and the time cost keeps growing.

Book a Free Call

Both choices can be reasonable

I'm a bookkeeper, and I still tell some owners to keep doing it themselves. If the file is simple and you actually close the month, hiring me would be a convenience, not a rescue. This page is the comparison I would want if I were deciding with my own money.

When DIY still makes sense

Stay with it if most of these are true:

  • You have one or two bank accounts and a card, and you know which is which.
  • Transactions are modest enough that a monthly reconciliation is a short task, not a project.
  • Personal spending stays out of the business accounts, or you pull it out every month.
  • You can explain last month’s profit without opening the bank app and guessing.
  • Your tax preparer isn't waiting on a reconstruction every spring.

If that's you, and you only want a cleaner setup, QuickBooks Online training is a better fit than handing the books over. I'd rather teach you a routine you'll keep than sell you a monthly plan you don't need.

When DIY starts costing more than it saves

The fee is the obvious cost of hiring a bookkeeper. The quieter cost of DIY is the month you never close. I see the same pattern with contractors, home-service companies, and professional practices: the work on the job is busy, and the bookkeeping waits.

  • You can't name the last month that was reconciled.
  • The bank balance in the software and the balance at the bank are different, and you've stopped chasing the difference.
  • Draws, personal charges, and job costs are mixed, so profit is a feeling.
  • You avoid quoting a job, hiring, or moving money because you don't trust the books.
  • A lender or a tax preparer asked for financial statements you can't produce.

At that point the choice isn't “free versus $350.” It's a few more months of drift, then a cleanup project, versus getting current and staying there. Catch-up is priced from the periods and the mess, so letting it stack is rarely the cheaper path.

What I take over, and what stays with you

On monthly bookkeeping, I categorize the transactions, reconcile the accounts in scope, and send the reports. You still own the decisions: what a transfer was, whether a charge was personal, and which job a cost belonged to. I can't invent those answers from a bank feed.

You also still have a tax preparer if you want one. I don't prepare or file the tax return. I can make the handoff less painful by keeping the year in order.

If the file is already behind, we start with cleanup, then move to a monthly rhythm. The checklist of what to gather is short on purpose. You don't need every statement in hand before the first call.

A practical way to decide

Look at the last three months. If each one was reconciled and you used the profit and loss statement, keep going. If you're reconstructing those months from memory, call me.

Common questions

Can I take the books back later if I want to do them myself again?

Yes. The QuickBooks file stays yours. If you want it back, I'll leave the reconciliations current and show you where things stand. You aren't locked in just to keep your own history.

How will I know the categories are right if I'm not clicking them?

You still get the profit and loss statement and the balance sheet, and you can question any line. If a number looks wrong, say so. I'd rather fix a category than have you nod at a report you don't believe.

What if some months are already closed and others are a mess?

We don't have to throw out the months you already trust. We'll start from the last closed month and rebuild forward. Finished work can stay finished.

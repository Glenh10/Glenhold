Books your CPA can use

I hand your CPA clean, reconciled books. They file the return. You keep the preparer you trust, and the year arrives as closed months.

Book a Free Call

I hand your CPA clean, reconciled books. They file the return. That is the whole relationship. You keep the preparer you trust. I make the year a set of closed months instead of a folder of statements and a question mark.

Clean, for this handoff, means the bank and the cards are reconciled through the period they asked for, the profit and loss covers that period, and the balance sheet ties to those reconciliations. Owner draws and contributions are labeled. Sales tax you collected is a liability. Personal charges that slipped onto a business card are identified. I send that package, and I stay available for the questions your preparer asks about a line.

California items show up in almost every handoff I do. The statewide sales-tax base rate is 7.25 percent, and district taxes can raise the rate in your city. I look up the rate and I record what you collected. Most California LLCs owe an $800 minimum franchise tax each year. A first-year waiver covered only LLCs registered in 2021, 2022, and 2023. The LLC fee has historically started when total income reaches $250,000. Your CPA confirms the current FTB rule and files the form. I keep the books those forms are built from.

If the year is behind, the handoff starts as a catch-up. I quote the open months, close them, and then the monthly plan keeps the next year from arriving in the same shape. Tell me the preparer's deadline on the first call so the quote respects it. I've been in accounting since 2021, including audit work at PwC, and I'm a Certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor. Now I bring that same care to small businesses around Simi Valley.

Your preparer may also ask for a general ledger, a detail of fixed assets, or the payroll reports from your payroll company. Send me that list with the deadline. I would rather assemble what they named than guess at a package.

Getting a year ready for your preparer

  1. Ask what period they need. Usually the calendar year, sometimes a fiscal year. I follow their date.
  2. Close each month in that period. Categories and reconciliations, month by month, so the year is a stack of finished months.
  3. Label owner and tax items. Draws, contributions, sales tax collected, and payroll drafts.
  4. Send the reports and the leftover list. So the preparer sees both the numbers and the open questions.
  5. Answer their follow-ups. You can loop me in. I would rather explain a deposit than leave it guessed.

What your preparer is glad to receive

  • Reconciled bank and card accounts
  • A profit and loss for the year
  • A year-end balance sheet
  • Payroll reports from your payroll company
  • A note of anything still estimated

Common questions

Do you file the tax return?

Your CPA files the return. I hand them clean, reconciled books.

What does clean mean?

Reconciled accounts, a profit and loss, a balance sheet, and owner activity labeled for the period they asked for.

Can you work from their checklist?

Yes. Send me the list and the date. I will quote the months that are still open.

How is sales tax shown?

Tax you collected sits as a liability. I look up your rate. Your preparer files the return.

What about the California $800 LLC tax?

Most LLCs owe it each year. I record the payment. Your CPA files the form and confirms the current rule.

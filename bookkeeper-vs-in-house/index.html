Outsourced Bookkeeper vs. Hiring an In-House Bookkeeper

An employee and an outsourced bookkeeper solve different problems. The right one depends on how much bookkeeping you actually have.

Book a Free Call

Start with the job, not the title

Owners ask me whether they should hire someone or send the books out. The useful question underneath is how many days of bookkeeping the business really has. A monthly close is one kind of job. A person who should be in the office all week, paying bills and answering customers, is another.

I do the first kind. You work directly with me. If you need the second kind, I will tell you that an employee is the better fit.

What outsourced bookkeeping is good at

Monthly bookkeeping is categorization, reconciliation, and reports. For a lot of contractors, home-service companies, and professional services firms, that work piles up once or twice a month. It does not fill forty hours every week.

Hiring a full-time person for that pile means you pay for the empty hours, or you invent other duties to justify the role. Sometimes those other duties are real. Often they are a second job taped onto bookkeeping, and the books are still the thing that slips.

With me, the monthly deliverable is specific: the accounts in scope reconciled, and a profit and loss statement and balance sheet you can use. Questions come to me, not to a shared inbox I never see.

When an in-house bookkeeper is the better hire

Hire in-house if someone needs to be physically present. Typical signs:

  • Bills should be entered and paid throughout the week, not in a monthly batch.
  • Customers walk in or call with payment questions that should be answered on the spot.
  • You want a person at a desk your crew already knows.
  • The bookkeeping, billing, and office work together really do fill the week.

That is a real job. I am not competing with it, and I am not going to talk you out of it if the days are there. What I can do first is catch the books up so a new hire is not spending their first quarter excavating old months.

The costs people forget

An advertised wage is not the cost of an employee. Add employer taxes, benefits if you offer them, the software seat, and the hours you spend recruiting and reviewing their work. Add the month they are out and nobody closes the books.

An outsourced plan has its own limits. I am not in your office. I will not run the front desk. I need statements and answers from you, and the close waits on those. If the relationship is not working, you should be able to stop without unwinding a hire. We agree on the scope before I start, and if monthly work ends, you keep the QuickBooks file and the reports.

Tax return preparation and filing sit outside both options unless the person you hire also does that work. I do not file returns. I keep books your tax preparer can rely on.

How I would choose

If the books need a few focused days a month, start with a monthly plan and skip the job posting. If you are already turning work away because nobody is in the office to handle money questions, hire. If you are unsure, describe a normal week. I will tell you which description you just gave me.

Common questions

Is an outsourced bookkeeper less expensive than an employee?

Often, when the work is a monthly close rather than a full-time job. An employee is a wage plus employer taxes, benefits, software, and coverage for time off. My monthly plans start at $350, $450, or $700 depending on scope. If you need a person in the office every day, an employee can be the better fit.

Who does the bookkeeping if I hire Glenhold?

You work directly with me. I do the bookkeeping, and you can call or email me when something looks off.

When is an in-house bookkeeper the better choice?

In-house is the better choice when bills, customer payments, and day-to-day questions need someone on site through the week, and there is enough of that work to fill the role.

Can you help before I hire an employee?

Yes. I can catch the books up and leave a file someone else can take over. If you hire later, that person inherits a system instead of a backlog.
